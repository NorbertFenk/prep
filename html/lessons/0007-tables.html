Tables that don't lie

Tables are for one thing: tabular data — information that means something in rows and columns. Not page layout; that era ended with modern CSS, and layout tables still confuse screen readers, produce tag soup, and refuse to be responsive.

The skeleton

<table>
  <caption>Data about the planets of our solar system</caption>
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <td colspan="2"></td>
      <th scope="col">Name</th>
      <th scope="col">Mass (10<sup>24</sup>kg)</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <th rowspan="4" colspan="2" scope="rowgroup">Terrestrial planets</th>
      <th scope="row">Mercury</th>
      <td>0.330</td>
    </tr>
    <!-- more rows -->
  </tbody>
</table>
1
Cells and rows

<tr> is a row; <td> a data cell; <th> a header cell — semantically a heading, not just bold text.

2
Structure

<thead>, <tbody> (and <tfoot>) group rows so screen readers and styling can address regions. <caption> titles the whole table.

3
Spanning

colspan="2" stretches a cell across two columns; rowspan="4" down four rows — how the "Terrestrial planets" group header covers its four planets.

4
colgroup/col

Style whole columns structurally: <colgroup><col span="2"><col class="column-border"><col span="9"></colgroup> draws a border around the planet-name column without touching a single <td>.

Why <th> and scope: the accessibility payoff

A sighted user reads a cell by glancing up and left. A screen reader user needs the associations declared. scope does it:

<th scope="col">Diameter (km)</th>      <!-- headers this column -->
<th scope="row">Mercury</th>            <!-- headers this row -->
<th scope="rowgroup">Terrestrial planets</th>  <!-- headers the row group -->

With correct headers and scopes, a screen reader can announce "Mercury, diameter: 4,879 kilometres" — or read out an entire column at once. (The id/headers pairing is the alternative for gnarly tables; scope is the one that's easy and covers most cases.)
