Structure: semantic page landmarks

Every page has the same anatomy: a header strip, navigation, the main content, maybe a sidebar, a footer. HTML has an element for each — and using them instead of anonymous <div>s is what turns pixels into a structure.

~30 min · prereq: 0002, 0008

The landmarks

<header>      <!-- introductory content for the page (or a section) -->
<nav>         <!-- the main navigation -->
<main>        <!-- the unique content of THIS page: one per page -->
  <article>   <!-- self-contained: makes sense on its own -->
  <section>   <!-- a thematic grouping; start it with a heading -->
  <aside>     <!-- tangential content: related links, author bio -->
</main>
<footer>      <!-- end content: fine print, credits, contacts -->
1
<main>: one per page

Directly inside <body>, not nested in other elements — it's exactly the part that makes this page this page.

2
<article> vs <section>

An article makes sense on its own (a blog post, a product card); a section is a themed piece of the page (a mini map, a set of headlines). They nest both ways, and sections should begin with a heading.

3
<header> and <footer> are contextual

Child of <body>: the global page header/footer. Child of an article: that article's own header/footer. Not the same thing as <title> or headings.

4
<div> and <span>: the honest fallbacks

When no semantic element fits and you need a hook for CSS or JS: <div> (block) or <span> (inline), usually with a class. They mean nothing — that's their job. But div-itis clutters documents; use them only when nothing better exists.

Why bother: the screen reader dividend

Assistive tech exposes landmarks as a menu: "find the main navigation", "jump to main content". With div soup, that menu is empty and users must wade through everything, every time. Semantics also feed SEO. The look changes at a designer's whim; the meaning — and the navigation built on it — survives.

Exercise — the real MDN challenge (Birdwatching site)

Take this unstructured page and add: a <header> wrapping the <h1> (with the dove logo inside it, empty alt) and the nav menu; a <main> containing an <article> (the welcome text) and an <aside> (four thumbnail images, each wrapped in a link to its full-size version, each with real alt text); and a <footer> for the credits:

Home Get started Photos Gear Forum

  wanting to share ideas, tips, and photos with other like-minded people.
</p>

<p>
