HTML / Lesson 6

Video and audio

Before HTML5, video meant plugins — Flash, QuickTime. Now two native elements do it, and the interesting parts are the attributes and the fallback habits.

~25 min · prereq: 0005

<video>

<video src="rabbit320.webm" controls>
  <p>
    Your browser doesn't support HTML video. Here is a
    <a href="rabbit320.webm">link to the video</a> instead.
  </p>
</video>
1
controls

Shows the browser's play/pause/volume UI. Users must be able to stop playback — autoplaying unsilent video is an accessibility failure, not just bad taste.

2
Fallback content

Anything inside the element that isn't <source> renders when the browser can't play the media at all — here, a download link.

3
width/height, poster

Size and aspect ratio as with images; poster supplies a splash image shown before playback starts.

4
autoplay, loop, muted, preload

Autoplay annoys — if you must autoplay, also muted (many browsers require it). loop restarts forever. preload buffers ahead: auto, metadata, or none.

Why multiple sources: containers and codecs

A media file is a container (WebM, MP4) holding tracks encoded by a codec (VP8/VP9, H.264…). Browsers support different sets — MP4/H.264 was patent-encumbered, WebM is open — so you offer both and let the browser choose:

<video controls>
  <source src="rabbit320.mp4" type="video/mp4" />
  <source src="rabbit320.webm" type="video/webm" />
  <p>Your browser doesn't support this video. Here is a
     <a href="rabbit320.mp4">link to the video</a> instead.</p>
</video>

The type attribute lets the browser skip formats it can't play without downloading them first. It plays the first source it supports, top to bottom.

<audio> and text tracks

<audio controls>
  <source src="viper.mp3" type="audio/mp3" />
  <source src="viper.ogg" type="audio/ogg" />
</audio>

<video controls width="320" height="240">
  <source src="video.mp4" type="video/mp4" />
  <track kind="subtitles" src="subtitles_en.vtt" srclang="en" label="English" />
</video>

<audio> works like <video> minus the visual bits — no width/height/poster. <track> attaches subtitles or captions (WebVTT files); srclang and label tell users which language they're picking.
