HTML / Lesson 5

Images and figures

You embedded an image in lesson 1 by copying an <img> tag. Today you learn what every attribute is for — including the one that decides whether blind visitors get the content or a filename read aloud.

~25 min · prereq: 0001, 0003

<img> and its attributes

<img src="images/dinosaur.jpg"
     alt="The head and torso of a dinosaur skeleton; a large head with long sharp teeth"
     width="400" height="341">
1
src — required

Path or URL to the image; relative for your own files, absolute only when the image lives elsewhere. Never hotlink someone else's image without permission — you'd be spending their bandwidth for content you don't control.

2
alt — the accessibility contract

A text stand-in for people who can't see the image: screen reader users, broken paths, disabled images, search engines. What you write depends on why the image exists — see below.

3
width/height — reserve the space

The page renders before images arrive. Declaring dimensions lets the browser reserve the right box, so content doesn't reflow and jolt when each image lands.

4
title — a tooltip

Hover-only supporting information. Never a substitute for alt — touch and keyboard users never see it.

Writing alt text: the decision tree

<img src="chart.png" alt="Quarterly revenue rose 40% between 2024 and 2026">  <!-- content -->
<img src="divider.png" alt="">   <!-- decoration: empty, not missing -->
<a href="report.pdf"><img src="icon.png" alt="Download the report"></a>  <!-- image as link -->
1
Content image? Describe the information it carries, briefly. If the surrounding text already says it, alt="" is honest.
2
Pure decoration? alt="" — empty, never missing. A missing alt makes many screen readers read the filename aloud; an empty alt is a deliberate "skip this".
3
Image inside a link? The alt becomes the link text: describe the destination, not the picture.

Captions: <figure> and <figcaption>

<figure>
  <img src="firefox.png"
       alt="An abstract flaming fox wrapping around a blue sphere"
       width="446" height="460">
  <figcaption>The 2019 Firefox logo</figcaption>
</figure>

<figure> semantically ties the image to its caption. Caption and alt do different jobs: alt is the invisible stand-in; figcaption is visible context that sits alongside — don't just repeat one in the other.
