Headings, paragraphs, and lists

Last lesson you wrote a page. This lesson you structure it — and learn the one idea that separates HTML writers from HTML copiers: semantics, meaning chosen for what content is, not how it should look.

Semantics: the right element for the right job

You could make any element look like a heading by wrapping it in a <span> with CSS. That gets you the pixels but nothing else. The <h1> element means "the top-level heading of this page" — and that meaning is used by screen readers (which navigate by headings), search engines (which boost keywords found in headings), and your future self. Style is CSS's job; structure is HTML's.

Headings

<h1> through <h6> form a document outline. Use one <h1>, nest the rest logically, and never skip levels (<h2> then <h4> breaks the outline). Don't pick a level because you like its font size — that's CSS territory.

Lists

<ul>
  <li>milk</li>
  <li>eggs</li>
</ul>

<ol>
  <li>Turn right</li>
  <li>Go across the roundabouts</li>
</ol>

<dl>
  <dt>soliloquy</dt>
  <dd>In drama, where a character speaks to themselves...</dd>
</dl>
1
<ul> — unordered

Items where order doesn't matter: shopping lists, feature lists — and the raw material of every navigation menu on the web.

2
<ol> — ordered

Steps where sequence matters: directions, recipes, rankings.

3
<dl> — description

Term/description pairs (<dt>/<dd>): glossaries, FAQs, metadata. You've been reading one in every reference doc of this workspace.

Exercise 1 — the real MDN task (Text basics 1)

Mark up this raw text with the right heading and paragraph elements:

Basic HTML Animals

This is the first paragraph in our page. It introduces our animals.

The Llama

Our Llama is a big fan of list items. When she spies a patch of them on a
web page, she will eat them like sweets, licking her lips as she goes.

The Anaconda

The crafty anaconda likes to slither around the page, traveling rapidly by
way of anchors to sneak up on his prey.

Do it, then compare:

Show solution 
<h1>Basic HTML Animals</h1>

<p>This is the first paragraph in our page. It introduces our animals.</p>

<h2>The Llama</h2>

<p>Our Llama is a big fan of list items. When she spies a patch of them on a
web page, she will eat them like sweets, licking her lips as she goes.</p>

<h2>The Anaconda</h2>

<p>The crafty anaconda likes to slither around the page, traveling rapidly by
way of anchors to sneak up on his prey.</p>

Exercise 3 — the real MDN task (Text basics 3)

Turn animals and definitions into a description list:

<h1>Advanced HTML Animals</h1>

Llama
Tall, woolly quadruped, pointy ears. Sometimes rideable, but grumpy and
spits a lot. Big fan of list items.
Anaconda
A very large constrictor snake; travels rapidly by way of anchors to sneak
up on his prey.
Hippopotamus
His description is bottomless.
Show solution 
<dl>
  <dt>Llama</dt>
  <dd>
    Tall, woolly quadruped, pointy ears. Sometimes rideable, but grumpy and
    spits a lot. Big fan of list items.
  </dd>
  <dt>Anaconda</dt>
  <dd>
    A very large constrictor snake; travels rapidly by way of anchors to
    sneak up on his prey.
  </dd>
  <dt>Hippopotamus</dt>
  <dd>His description is bottomless.</dd>
</dl>
