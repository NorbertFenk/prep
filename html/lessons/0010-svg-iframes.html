Media and embedding beyond the page

Sometimes the content isn't even a raster image or a video file: it's vector art, or an entire other web page. Three elements handle that — one of them is the most dangerous element in HTML.

~25 min · prereq: 0005, 0006

SVG: markup for graphics

<svg width="300" height="200">
  <rect width="100%" height="100%" fill="green" />
</svg>

SVG is XML-based markup describing shapes — <circle>, <rect>, <path>. It's vector: resolution independent, small for geometric art. Two ways to include it:

1
Via <img src="logo.svg">

Quick, cacheable, alt text works — but no CSS or JS access to the internals.

2
Inline in your HTML

The shapes become part of your DOM: styleable with CSS, animatable, focusable. Costs an HTTP-request saving but no caching, and bloats every page it's pasted into.

<iframe>: a page inside a page

<iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/VIDEO_ID"
        width="100%" height="500"
        allowfullscreen sandbox>
</iframe>

YouTube embeds, Google Maps, Disqus comments — an iframe embeds an entire document, with its own browsing context. That power is the problem: an iframe is a classic attack vector — invisible frames stealing clicks (clickjacking), embedded pages reading your cookies. The defenses:

1
sandbox — always

With no value, it blocks scripts, forms, popups — everything. Add permissions back one at a time, only as needed. (Never combine allow-scripts with allow-same-origin on same-origin content: the frame could then remove its own sandbox.)

2
HTTPS only

Encrypted transport protects the embedded content from tampering, and keeps embedded and parent documents apart.

3
Respect the server's no

Sites send X-Frame-Options/Content-Security-Policy headers to forbid being framed — that's why embedding MDN's glossary in an iframe shows a console error instead of the page. Only embed when necessary, with permission.

<embed> and <object>

Legacy embedding elements from the plugin era (Flash, Java applets) — both dead. Their remaining use cases (PDF viewing, external resources) are all better served by <iframe> and its security features. Recognize them; don't write them.
